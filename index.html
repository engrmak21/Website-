FAQ

Dry ice, handled safely.

What is dry ice? +

Dry ice is solid carbon dioxide, held at around −78.5°C. Unlike water ice, it doesn't melt into liquid — it sublimates, turning directly from solid into gas, which is why it stays "dry".

How should I store it? +

Use a well-insulated, ventilated container such as a cooler or Styrofoam box. Never store it in an airtight container — the gas released as it sublimates needs somewhere to go.

Is it safe to handle? +

Yes, with basic precautions: always use gloves or tongs, never touch it with bare skin, and work in a ventilated space, since the CO₂ gas released can build up in enclosed rooms.

Can it go in food or drinks? +

No — dry ice should never be swallowed or placed directly in food or beverages. For fog effects in drinks, it must stay fully separated from the liquid and be allowed to sublimate away before serving.

How long does it last? +

Roughly a few hours in open air, and 24–48 hours (sometimes longer) in a well-insulated cooler, depending on quantity and ambient temperature.

Can I transport it in my car? +

Yes — keep it in a ventilated, insulated box, secure it so it can't tip, and crack a window. Avoid keeping large quantities in a closed cabin for long periods.
